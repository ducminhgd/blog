**Greenland sharks can live for more than 400 years.**

The Greenland shark is among the longest-lived vertebrates ever discovered.

A 2016 study published in *Science* examined 28 female Greenland sharks using radiocarbon dating of proteins in their eye lenses.

Key findings:

- Estimated lifespan: at least 272 years.
- Largest specimen: estimated 392 years old, with an uncertainty range of ±120 years.
- Estimated age at sexual maturity: approximately 156 years.
- Growth rate: less than 1 cm per year.

The researchers used carbon-14 signatures, including the radioactive signal from nuclear testing in the 1960s, to estimate the sharks' ages.

**An interesting implication:** A Greenland shark alive today could have been born before the American Revolution (1776)
